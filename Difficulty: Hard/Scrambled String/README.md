<p dir="ltr" style="box-sizing: border-box; text-rendering: geometricprecision; -webkit-tap-highlight-color: transparent; color: #273239; font-family: Nunito, sans-serif; font-size: 18px; line-height: 1.2; margin: 0px 0px 10px; padding: 0px; border: 0px; vertical-align: baseline; background-color: #ffffff; letter-spacing: 0.131625px !important;"><span style="box-sizing: border-box; text-rendering: geometricprecision; -webkit-tap-highlight-color: transparent; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.2; margin: 0px; padding: 0px; border: 0px; vertical-align: baseline; letter-spacing: 0.131625px !important;"><strong style="box-sizing: border-box; text-rendering: geometricprecision; -webkit-tap-highlight-color: transparent; line-height: 1.2; margin: 0px; padding: 0px; border: 0px; vertical-align: baseline; letter-spacing: 0.131625px !important;">Note:</strong></span><span style="box-sizing: border-box; text-rendering: geometricprecision; -webkit-tap-highlight-color: transparent; font-size: inherit; color: inherit; font-weight: inherit; line-height: 1.6; letter-spacing: 0.162px !important;"> A scrambled string is different from an anagram.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-size: 18px;"><strong>Examples:&nbsp;</strong></span></p>
<pre><strong style="font-size: 18px;">Input: </strong><span style="font-size: 18px;">s1 = "coder", s2 = "ocder" </span><strong style="font-size: 18px;">
Output: </strong><span style="font-size: 18px;">true </span><span style="font-size: 18px;"><strong>
Explanation: </strong></span><span style="font-size: 18px;">Split the string "coder" into two substrings, "co" and "der". Then, split "co" into "c" and "o" and swap them to obtain "oc". Combining the scrambled substring "oc" with "der" gives "ocder". Therefore, "ocder" is a scrambled form of "coder".</span></pre>
<pre><span style="font-size: 18px;"><strong>Input: </strong>s1 = "abcde", s2 = "caebd" <strong>

Explanation: </strong>"caebd" is not a scrambled form of "abcde".</span></pre>
